> **Draft for 1.3.0 (unreleased).** The figures below were measured on the 1.3.0
> candidate (main `edfdb49`) with the benchmark rig of ADR 0090. They are re-confirmed on
> the release candidate before the tag.

## What is new

- **Durable QoS 1 skips the disk when the subscriber keeps up**
  ([ADR 0089](https://github.com/mbilling/fss-mqtt-broker/blob/main/docs/adr/0089-durable-write-elision.md)).
  - **The mechanism:** a delivery to an online persistent subscriber goes out at once and
    is written only if its PUBACK does not arrive within `durable.write_elision_ms`, now
    50 ms by default.
  - **The guarantee is unchanged:** the publisher's ack still waits for the subscriber's
    PUBACK or the quorum write, whichever comes first, so an acknowledged message is
    never lost.
  - **Faults and order:** a node that dies inside the window has acknowledged nobody, so
    the publisher resends. Held deliveries on a topic are written before any later
    durable delivery on it, so a replay keeps the order.
  - **Slow subscribers:** one that keeps missing the window is written directly for a
    while.
  - **Turning it off:** `durable.write_elision_ms = 0`.
- **The segment log replica store is the default for new data dirs**
  ([ADR 0078](https://github.com/mbilling/fss-mqtt-broker/blob/main/docs/adr/0078-replica-segment-log.md)).
  - **Who gets it:** a fresh data dir is created as an append-only segment log.
  - **Existing data dirs:** they keep the store they have (see Upgrading).
- **Measured with the 1.3.0 defaults** (3 rounds a step; a step counts only if no round
  lost a message; [every step](https://github.com/mbilling/fss-mqtt-broker/blob/main/docs/benchmarks/RIG2-RESULTS.md)).
  Maximum lossless msg/s:

  | workload | 3 nodes | 10 nodes |
  |---|---|---|
  | durable QoS 1, ack after fsync and a quorum copy | 117,800 (114,200 at ack p99 ≤ 1 s) | 292,200 (228,000 at ack p99 ≤ 1 s) |
  | QoS 1, clean sessions | 270,800 | 875,900 |
  | QoS 0 | 340,300 | 973,200 |

- **One unreadable durable session no longer holds a node's QoS 1 acks** (#1049).
  - **Before:** a node that owned a session it could not read (`no replication quorum`)
    held the PUBACK of every QoS 1 publish matching no session it knew, for as long as
    the read kept failing.
  - **Now:** after two minutes of failed scans the session is quarantined, named at WARN,
    counted on `mqttd_sessions_quarantined` and listed on `/statusz`
    (`session_quarantine`), and the held acks are released. The quarantine lifts when
    the session reads again.
  - **The cost:** while it lasts, a publish whose only match is a subscription of that
    session the node never read is acked and not stored for it. The session could not
    store it anyway. See `docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md`.

- **Relayed sessions end with the cluster certificates they are relayed under** (#1016).
  - **Before:** a persistent session relayed to its owner (ADR 0005) ran on a cluster-bus
    connection that neither the expiry check nor the cluster CRL's sweep looked at. It
    outlived the certificates it was made with, and their revocation; a peer link does not.
  - **Now:** both nodes hold the relay to the certificates it was made with. It ends within
    a second of the earlier `notAfter`, and at once when a reload's cluster CRL revokes
    the other node. Counted as `mqttd_revocation_evictions_total{reason=}`
    `relay-peer-cert-expired`, `relay-own-cert-expired` and `relay-peer-revoked`. The
    landing node now also checks the owner's certificate against the CRL and its end
    before relaying.
  - **The cost:** a certificate renewal reconnects each relayed client once (v5:
    DISCONNECT `0x9C`), and its Will is published. The reconnects are spread over a few
    minutes after the reload, or over a window before the old end, not all in one
    second. See `docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "Relayed sessions reconnect once per renewal".

- **A broker out of reach for up to 3 s is no longer declared dead**
  ([ADR 0016](https://github.com/mbilling/fss-mqtt-broker/blob/main/docs/adr/0016-swim-membership-stability.md),
  amendment of 2026-10-25; issue #975).
  - **What changed:** a node told by another that a member is dead asks that member
    once, directly, before it agrees, as it already did before its own verdict; and the
    suspicion window no longer shortens below 2.5 s (1.5 s in 1.2.0) when several nodes
    suspect the same member.
  - **What it measured** (simulation, 32 runs a cell): a member cut off for 2.5 s or
    3 s is declared dead in no run at 3 to 30 nodes, against up to 8 runs of 32 on
    1.2.0; the bound is 3 s less twice the network's latency, from 2 s.
  - **The price:** a broker that really died is held dead by every other node about
    0.4 to 0.8 s later than on 1.2.0 (median 4.9 s at 3 nodes to 7.4 s at 30).
  - **Nothing to configure, nothing on the wire:** the detector's timings are
    constants of the release, and the check is an ordinary probe.

- **mqtt-bridge no longer strands a message in its spool when a side reconnects** (#1100).
  - **Before:** a message forwarded toward a side at the moment that side connected could
    be written to the spool just after the connect-time replay. Its source was already
    acked, and it waited until the next reconnect, or was lost with the spool.
  - **Now:** the choice between sending live and spooling is made under the same lock as
    the connect and disconnect handoff, so a message is either replayed or sent live.

- **A subscriber far behind no longer loses messages** (#1114).
  - **Before (since v1.0.8):** when more than 1,024 packets were queued for one
    connection, one packet in every 1,025 was dropped unwritten. An acknowledged QoS 1
    message could vanish, still counted as delivered, with no drop counter moving.
  - **Now:** every queued packet is written. Found by the new benchmark rig's identity
    ledger.

- **A QoS 1 subscriber with a large window can no longer freeze its connection** (#1117).
  - **Before (since v1.0.8, #443):** a connection awaited each batch of up to 1,024 packets
    in place and read one packet per turn in between. A subscriber that writes its PUBACKs
    before it reads again then filled both directions' buffers, and each side blocked
    writing to the other. The subscriber stayed connected and received nothing, holding
    up to 65,535 messages in flight and a full backlog. Its keepalive was not checked
    while the write was blocked.
  - **Now:** a connection keeps reading while a write to it is blocked, over TCP, TLS,
    WebSocket and QUIC, and its keepalive is checked throughout. Order on the wire is
    unchanged.
  - **A peer that stops reading for good is closed:** once it has taken no bytes for
    `limits.write_stall_keepalives` keepalive periods (default 2), or
    `limits.write_stall_timeout_secs` with keepalive 0 (default 120). Counted as
    `mqttd_connection_errors_total{reason="write-stall"}`; its Will is published.

- **A durable session whose connection ended without a detach expires on time** (#1118).
  - **Before:** a session's expiry deadline was written only when its connection
    detached. A broker that crashed or was killed with the client connected, or a
    deadline write the store refused during an ownership move ("not the owning node"),
    left a session record with no deadline. The restarted node or the new owner then
    kept that session, its subscriptions and its offline queue for good.
  - **Now:** the Session Expiry Interval is stored with the session when the client
    connects. A node that inherits a session without a deadline expires it that many
    seconds later (plus 10 s in a cluster, in case the session is still moving), which
    is no earlier than the spec allows. A v3.1.1 `clean_session=0` session still never
    expires.
  - **Sessions written by 1.2.0** carry no interval and keep the 1.2.0 behaviour until
    their client connects to a 1.3.0 node once.

- **A resume is served by the session's owner whatever its expiry, also during startup**
  (#1119).
  - **Before:** an MQTT 5 client that connected with `clean_start=false` and Session
    Expiry 0, which is legal, stayed on the node it reached. On every node that did not
    own its client id, it was refused "server unavailable" (`0x88`) after 5 s, even for a
    brand-new id. In a cluster's first seconds, before ownership was settled, any resume
    could be refused the same way.
  - **Now:** every CONNECT that may resume a session is relayed to the session's owner.
    On a durable cluster, a CONNECT that arrives before its owner is known waits for it,
    for up to 5 s, and is then relayed there.

## Upgrading from 1.2.0

A rolling upgrade, one node at a time (ADR 0039), with `scripts/roll-upgrade.sh`,
`helm upgrade` or the operator.

- **Nothing changes on disk.** A node whose data dir already holds a redb replica store
  keeps it, and logs which store it opened and why. Rolling back to 1.2.0 works as
  before.
- **Session records gain the expiry interval** (#1118), at their end. 1.2.0 reads them
  and ignores it, so a mixed cluster and a rollback work. A session a 1.2.0 node rewrites
  loses the interval again until its client next connects to a 1.3.0 node.
- **Moving an existing node to the segment log** is a deliberate step:
  1. Set `MQTTD_REPLICA_STORE=log` and restart. The redb store is imported once, and its
     files are renamed `*.imported` (remove them when you are satisfied).
  2. **There is no conversion back:** 1.2.x refuses to start on a segment-log data dir.
     To return such a node to 1.2.x, wipe its data dir and let it rejoin (it is
     re-replicated from its peers), or restore it from a backup.

  A node rebuilt with a fresh disk (a new Kubernetes volume, a replaced VM) gets the
  segment log on its own. A cluster whose nodes use different stores is fine: the store
  is per node.
- **Failure detection during the roll.** A 1.2.0 node still declares a member dead
  on the shorter window, and adopts another node's verdict without asking; an
  upgraded node checks such a verdict before it agrees. While the roll is in progress
  an outage of about 3 s can still end in a dead verdict on the 1.2.0 nodes (in
  simulation at 14 nodes: 8 runs of 32 with every node on 1.2.0, 7 with half upgraded,
  3 with one left, none after).
- **Write elision is on.** To keep the 1.2.0 behaviour, set
  `durable.write_elision_ms = 0` (`MQTTD_DURABLE_WRITE_ELISION_MS=0`). What to watch is in
  `docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "Write elision on the durable QoS 1 path".

## Known limits

- **When elision helps:** only subscribers that are online and keep up. An offline or
  slower-than-the-window subscriber pays the write (and, for its first misses, the window
  on top) as before.
- **A `$share` start-up overflow** (#1078): on a cluster's first loaded seconds, a cold
  persistent `$share` member can fill its append lane and withhold acknowledgements.
  Nothing acknowledged is lost, and the publishers retry.
- **One durable session tops out at about 2,500 msg/s** (#1134). Its append lane keeps 16
  writes in flight, so its rate is 16 ÷ the append latency. Past that, its lane fills, and
  the publishers routed to it get their acknowledgements withheld and retry. Nothing
  acknowledged is lost. Spread a hot topic over a `$share` group: the cluster figures
  above put about 350 msg/s on each session. ADR 0091 T4 removes the ceiling.

The limits below are carried over from 1.2.0 unchanged. The
[1.2.0 notes](1.2.0.md#known-limits-of-this-release) give the detail.

- **A node that wrongly holds a live member dead can ack QoS 1 and 2 publishes it never
  delivers to that member's subscribers** (#973). This happens when the member stays out
  of reach for 8 s with durable sessions on, or when another subscription to the topic
  appears meanwhile. 1.3.0 makes a wrong verdict rarer: a cut of up to about 3 s no
  longer causes one (#975). The full fix (ADR 0087 T5) is designed and not built.
- **A minority side of a partition can ack a durable QoS 1 publish and lose it at the
  heal** (#1030). A node that is down, or one lost link, does not cause it.
- **A durable session that stays unreadable is quarantined, not repaired** (#1049). Why its
  placement group stops recovering after heavy churn is not found yet. The recovery
  warnings now name the check that refuses it (`refusal=`).
- **QoS 1 messages re-sent after a node dies, or after a session moves to its owner,
  arrive as new messages** (#1058): `DUP` 0 and a new packet id. Nothing is lost.
- **During a session's ownership move, a publish the old owner refuses may already have
  reached the new owner's live subscribers** (#1054). Each retry reaches them again,
  usually for under a second. Nothing is lost.
- **mqtt-bridge loses a QoS 1 forward it has sent but not yet seen acknowledged when that
  side disconnects** (#1103). The bridge keeps no in-flight state, and the source was
  acked when the forward was sent. The same window was already documented for a crash
  of the bridge (ADR 0060 T8).
- **Rule state is experimental, because it is in memory.** A restarted node comes back
  empty, and when every node stops, the state is gone. `strong` consistency is not built.
