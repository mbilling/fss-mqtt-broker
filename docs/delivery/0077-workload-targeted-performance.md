    status: done

RESULT: 1 site 30,000/s delivered exactly at p99 <=1ms; 2 sites 60,000/s exactly at p99 <=25ms; 4 sites delivered 119,636 of 120,000 (99.7%) but at p99 <=2000ms; 8 sites collapsed to 32,870 of 240,000 (24%) at p99 >30s. That is the ADR 0048/#447 single hub-thread ceiling, reproduced here on a completely different shape from the 2026-08-26 run that found it, and it makes #447 the lever that moves sites-per-node: the aggregate headroom says a node should carry roughly twice what it does. SCOPE, disclosed: one node, one rep per rung, one QoS, no bridge in the path, and the 1% QoS 1 alarm traffic is NOT measured here. The ladder's ceiling of 8 is the HARNESS's, not the broker's (16 sites needs 12 one-vCPU containers on the busiest of 5 drivers against 8 vCPUs; 32 sites needs 16 drivers) — the 32-rung ladder this task was originally written around does not exist and the shape check refuses it before a cluster is paid for."
  - id: 0077-T4

Result, labelled rung -> per subscriber: 20,000 -> 2,000 both QoS; 50,000 -> 5,000 both; 100,000 -> 9,978 at QoS 0 and 9,956 at QoS 1. Ten times the ~1,000 that run-curve.sh asserted and that sizing advice rested on. VALIDITY: pub_overrun was 0 for QoS 0 through the 100,000 rung, so publishers held schedule and the delivered rate is the broker's, not a driver artifact; the broker's own counters show received == delivered at every rung, which is what a shared group should do. HYPOTHESIS FALSIFIED: I predicted QoS 1 would reproduce the ~1,000 because a delivery there costs a PUBACK round trip and is gated by receive-maximum. It cleared it as easily as QoS 0, so the ceiling was not an acknowledgement cost. What the old runs hit was a limit elsewhere in their shape — 300 subscribers against thousands of publishers is the fan-in that PENDING_PUBLISH_CAP stalls, the same limit that voided telematics twice. The error was arithmetic: 'delivered / subscribers' is a QUOTIENT, and reading it as a per-subscriber ceiling assumed the subscribers were the constraint, which nothing in those runs established. CONSEQUENCE: a SCADA site at 30,000 msg/s needs 2-4 shared consumers, not 30 — ~12,000 datalake writers across a 3,000-site estate rather than 90,000. Claim corrected at its source in run-curve.sh and in workloads/README.md."
  - id: 0077-T9
    title: "The driver-side `sent` counter reads 2x the offered rate, and it silently suppresses knee detection"
    status: done
    date: 2026-08-29
    evidence: "Root cause is upstream, in emqtt-bench 0.6.3 itself. `publish/2` increments `pub` when `emqtt:publish` returns a bare `ok` (emqtt_bench.erl:913), and its caller in `loop/5` matches that same `ok` and increments `pub` AGAIN (:713). At QoS > 0 the return carries a reason code, so the callee increments `pub_succ` instead and `pub` is touched once. Hence exactly 2x at QoS 0 and 1x at QoS 1 — the prediction was tested on both arms before any fix: QoS 0 read 2.00x / 2.00x / 2.00x at the 20k/50k/100k rungs, QoS 1 read 1.00x / 1.00x / 0.99x. The correction is therefore one expression that needs no QoS branch, `(pub + pub_succ) / 2`, since pub_succ is 0 at QoS 0 and equals pub at QoS 1; it returns 1.00x at all six points. The blast radius was NARROWER than this task first claimed: `sustained` is recv >= 0.99 * sent, so a doubled sent only fails where recv is close to sent. market-data is fan-out (recv/sent = 240/2 = 120) and telematics is QoS 1 (1/1), so both cleared the test regardless — no published curve was wrong. Only QoS 0 + $share breaks (1/2 = 0.5), which is the T7 probe, and which is precisely the SCADA telemetry shape T3-T5 depend on. With the fix the T7 run's knee resolves at 100,000 msg/s offered (99,778/s delivered) and its latency becomes readable: p99 <=1ms at both 20k and 50k, <=1000ms at 100k, >30s at 200k. Regression-pinned by `summarize-curve.py --self-test` against synthesized driver logs (six cases, no cluster, no cost), wired into ci.yml beside the hygiene gate's own self-test; reverting the division fails it. AUDIT of everything already published, since the corrupted value fed three consumers: (1) `sent` is never PRINTED — the lane B table shows the rung label and the recv rate — so no published figure carried it. (2) `sustained` — only QoS 0 + $share, never published. (3) `offer_not_met` is sent_rate < 0.97*offered, which a doubled sent makes far too permissive; sweeping all 208 lane B rungs on disk, exactly TWO change their flag, both the rung-1000000-plain of the v1.0.8/v1.0.9 pair, which had a genuine driver shortfall (67% and 61% of offer) that went unflagged. (4) `late_share` divides by sent_rate and so was HALVED at QoS 0, but report_html renders flags only as a hover tooltip, never as a number. The +28.7% v1.0.8->v1.0.9 claim on the published curves page is NOT affected: it is computed from broker-side Prometheus counters (received + delivered, differenced across all 10 brokers), and reproduces at 1.30x from the raw counters against the published 1.29x. The newly visible shortfall corroborates rather than undermines it — v1.0.9 was offered 8% LESS load and did 30% MORE broker work at equal CPU (87.8% vs 88.7%) with fewer late publishes (18% vs 31%), the shortfall itself being a symptom of publish backpressure. What the audit DOES surface is a disclosure gap on that page rather than a wrong number: both runs shed ~45% of intended deliveries (pending-cap + outbound-full; 11.8M and 14.8M dropped), so the headline measures throughput under saturation and does not say so."
  - id: 0077-T8
    title: "Soak and node-kill — the two gaps that block every high-availability claim"
    status: planned
    notes: "Nothing runs longer than about a minute, so there is no evidence about sustained behaviour, memory growth or a 24-hour soak; and no node is killed and no network partitioned while load is running, so the HA claim rests on unit tests rather than on the rig. For the industries this ADR targets, behaviour on node loss is the FIRST question asked and currently has no measured answer. Expensive (hours, not minutes) and therefore sequenced last, but the report footer must keep disclosing the gap until this lands."

## Why these tasks, in this order

T1 and T2 are done and are the reason the rest is worth running: without a generated
report that carries tail latency, and without the four harness fixes, another campaign
would produce more plausible wrong numbers rather than more evidence.

T7 comes next despite being last-numbered in the SCADA story, because it is the
cheapest measurement here and the only one whose answer changes an architecture
before it is built — 3,000 datalake writers or 90,000 is not a tuning decision.

T3 → T4 → T5 is the SCADA sequence proper: build the lane, get sites-per-node, then
find out whether the daily burst is survivable. T6 answers the other half of the same
architecture question, and can run in parallel with T4 once T3 exists.

T8 is sequenced last because it is the most expensive, not because it is the least
important. Until it lands, every high-availability statement about this broker is
unmeasured, and the report says so on its face.

## The standard each task is held to

A task is `done` when its number is published in the generated report **with its tail
latency, its replication count, and its coverage gap** — not when the measurement
runs. A task that measures something and finds the shape unsuitable is `cut` with the
finding kept, following ADR 0076, where two of three tasks shipped as falsifications.
