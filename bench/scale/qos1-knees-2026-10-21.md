# QoS 1 knees at 3 and 10 nodes on the 1.2.0 release candidate — one provisioning

Campaign card. Env: [`qos1-campaign/confirm-knees-ec40721.env`](qos1-campaign/confirm-knees-ec40721.env).
Driver: [`482-per-node-knee.sh`](482-per-node-knee.sh). Method:
[`482-per-node-knee.md`](482-per-node-knee.md). The previous confirmation is
[`qos1-knees-2026-10-12.md`](qos1-knees-2026-10-12.md).

**The binary is an unreleased candidate.** `main` at `ec407218` (everything in 1.2.0),
built with the release recipe (`scripts/release/build-repro.sh x86_64-unknown-linux-musl
mqttd`, Ubuntu 24.04 amd64, Rust 1.97.0), stripped, jq embedded. Published as the
pre-release `bench-candidate-ec40721`, sha256 `e3c3dcbf…e036`. Not the signed release
pipeline's build. The run is stamped `UNRELEASED-BINARY.txt`.

## The question

Does the release candidate still carry the QoS 1 rates of 2026-10-12, after #1052–#1060
(including the takeover hold of #1053 and the resume fix of #1057)? Arm 3 answers it on
the same hosts by running the 2026-10-12 build (`7d4eced`, sha256 `9111c1eb…c513`).

## The run

- Run `.runs/knee-20261020T234848Z` (untracked), 2026-10-20 23:48 to 2026-10-21 01:38
  UTC, Hetzner `fsn1`. 10 × CCX23 brokers (4 dedicated vCPU) + 20 × CCX33 drivers, one
  provisioning, torn down and leak-audited by the rig ("nothing leaked").
- Arms on the same hosts: 10 nodes (sites 1 · 13 · 14 · 15), 3 nodes with 8 drivers
  (1 · 4 · 4 · 5), 10 nodes on the 2026-10-12 build (1 · 13), 10 nodes again (1 · 13)
  as the drift control. Each arm closes with a repeated 1-site control.
- A site is 3,000 QoS 1 publishers at 10 msg/s (30,000 msg/s, 200 B) into a `$share`
  group of 20 clean-session QoS 1 consumers. 60 s windows.
- Audit driver: emqtt-bench 0.6.3 + `qos1-driver/`, rebuilt for this run (archive sha256
  `d765e908…`, image `sha256:ab924082…`).
- `OBSERVE=1`.

## Result

asserted from the brokers' counters. The rate is each broker's
`mqttd_publish_delivered_total` over its own window (band at most ±0.02%). The rig's
verdict is the stricter certification, printed beside it.

Every 1-site rung (30,000 msg/s; eight of them) is GREEN and holds, p99 ≤ 5 ms.
**323,555,538 messages ledgered, none missing, unacknowledged or duplicated.** No
publish was forwarded between brokers on any rung of any arm (crossing gate PASS,
max 0.00%).

### What it shows

- **No regression since 2026-10-12.** On the same hosts, the 2026-10-12 build and the
  release candidate deliver the same at 10 nodes and 13 sites (390,005 and 390,004
  msg/s, both p99 ≤ 25 ms). The drift control (389,998) matches arm 1.
- **10 nodes hold 15 sites: 45,000 msg/s per node, 450,000 msg/s.** The 2026-10-12
  ladder stopped at 14.
- **3 nodes: 40,000 msg/s per node certified (GREEN), 50,000 at the top of the ladder,
  also GREEN.** 5 sites is where the ladder stopped, not the cluster.

### Why the 10-node rungs are not certified

The drivers' own delivery reading (subscriber logs; the audited evidence was refused
for endpoint scrape window uncertainty above 2%) came in 2.6–5.4% below the brokers'
counters. The rig does not certify a rung whose two readings disagree past 2%. The same
happened on 2026-10-12. The brokers' counters and the identity ledger close exactly, so
the reading holds; the certification does not.
