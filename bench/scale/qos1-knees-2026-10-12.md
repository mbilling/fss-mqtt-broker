# QoS 1 knees at 3 and 10 nodes on the 1.2.0 candidate — one provisioning

Campaign card. Env: [`qos1-campaign/confirm-knees-7d4eced.env`](qos1-campaign/confirm-knees-7d4eced.env).
Driver: [`482-per-node-knee.sh`](482-per-node-knee.sh). Method and harness safeguards:
[`482-per-node-knee.md`](482-per-node-knee.md). The durable arm of the same day is
[`durable-knees-2026-10-12.md`](durable-knees-2026-10-12.md).

**The binary is an unreleased candidate, not the signed release.** It is the
`release: v1.2.0` commit `7d4eced8`, cross-built by hand (zig cc, x86_64 musl, not
stripped, built without the jq module), published as the pre-release
`bench-candidate-7d4eced`, sha256 `9111c1eb…c513`. v1.2.0 was not tagged when this ran, and
this is not the release pipeline's build. The run is stamped `UNRELEASED-BINARY.txt`: its
numbers belong to that binary and are **not published-curve points**
([README](README.md), ADR 0048).

## The question

Does the release commit still carry the QoS 1 rates certified on earlier releases:
4 sites (40k msg/s per node) at 3 nodes on v1.0.17, and 13 sites (39k/node) at 10 nodes
on v1.0.18?

This is a confirmation on one binary. **No v1.1.0 arm ran on the same hosts**, so the run
does not measure a change since v1.1.0, in either direction.

## The run

- Run `.runs/knee-20261012T105735Z` (untracked), 2026-10-12, 10:57–12:21 UTC, Hetzner
  `fsn1`. 10 × CCX23 brokers (4 dedicated vCPU) + 20 × CCX33 drivers (8 dedicated vCPU),
  one provisioning, torn down and leak-audited by the rig.
- Arms on the same hosts: 10 nodes (sites 1 · 13 · 14; 15 skipped by the stop rule), 3
  nodes with 8 drivers (1 · 4 · 4 · 5), 10 nodes again (1 · 13) as the drift control. Each
  arm closes with a repeated 1-site control.
- A site is 3,000 QoS 1 publishers at 10 msg/s (30,000 msg/s, 200 B) into a `$share` group
  of 20 clean-session QoS 1 consumers. 60 s windows.
- Audit driver: emqtt-bench 0.6.3 + the `qos1-driver/` audit extension, rebuilt for this run
  (archive sha256 `2cded28d…`, image `sha256:1194c6b2…` on every container of every rung).
  It is not the `67bb4194…` archive behind the published curve.
- `OBSERVE=1`: live Grafana was attached, so every host also ran the metrics shipper. The
  rig's own note says a published number may want `OBSERVE=0`.

## Result

Every rung is read twice, and the two readings are kept apart.

- **The reading rule: a rung holds if no message was lost and p99 is at or below 1 s,
  asserted from the brokers' counters.** No loss means the rung's identity ledger closes
  and no broker dropped anything. The rate is the brokers' own: each broker's
  `mqttd_publish_delivered_total` over its own window. This is a reading, easy to state
  and to check. It is not a certification.
- **The rig's verdict** is the certification. GREEN also needs the offer met on schedule,
  a steady window, a load generator with headroom and valid driver-side evidence.

`summarize-curve.py` prints both: the verdict in its first table, the reading in
"Reading from the brokers' counters". In the table below, `delivered/s (brokers)` is the
figure the reading asserts. Every broker answered both window scrapes within 26 ms, so
that rate is known to ±0.03% on every rung. `delivered/s (drivers)` is the driver-side
figure the rig graded. Latencies are histogram bucket upper bounds, from the consumers.

| nodes | sites | offered msg/s | delivered/s (brokers) | per node | delivered/s (drivers) | p50 · p95 · p99 | reading | the rig's verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | 120,000 | 120,004 | 40.0k | 120,001 | ≤1 · ≤5 · ≤10 ms | holds | GREEN |
| 3 | 4 (rep 2) | 120,000 | 120,001 | 40.0k | 120,000 | ≤1 · ≤5 · ≤10 ms | holds | GREEN |
| 3 | 5 | 150,000 | 149,982 | 50.0k | 149,988 | ≤5 · ≤25 · ≤25 ms | holds | GREEN |
| 10 | 13 | 390,000 | 389,816 | 39.0k | 374,784 | ≤1 · ≤5 · ≤50 ms | holds | NOT CARRIED |
| 10 | 14 | 420,000 | 420,004 | 42.0k | 407,008 | ≤1 · ≤10 · ≤50 ms | holds | NOT CARRIED |
| 10 | 13 (drift control) | 390,000 | 390,290 | 39.0k | 376,125 | ≤1 · ≤5 · ≤25 ms | holds | NOT CARRIED |

Every 1-site rung (30,000 msg/s; six of them, opening and control of each arm) is GREEN
and holds, at p50 · p95 · p99 ≤ 1 ms.

### What held

- **3 nodes: 40.0k msg/s per node twice and 50.0k once, certified by the rig.** 50k is a
  floor: 5 sites is where the ladder stopped, not where the cluster did.
- **10 nodes: 39.0k and 42.0k msg/s per node hold** (390,000 msg/s twice, 420,000 once):
  nothing lost, p99 ≤ 50 ms, the brokers delivered the full offer. Not certified by the
  rig, for the reasons below.
- **No publish was forwarded between brokers**: 0.00% on every broker of every rung.
  `GATE nodes=3 PASS 5 rungs, cert=canary, max broker crossing 0.00% <= 0.5%`, and the
  same PASS on both 10-node arms.
- The ladder stopped after two consecutive NOT CARRIED rungs and skipped 15 sites
  (45k/node). The 10-node knee was not located.

### Why the rig did not certify the 10-node rungs

The verdicts, verbatim, in the order of the table:

```text
NOT CARRIED: INVALID EVIDENCE (endpoint scrape window uncertainty exceeds 2%); DRIVER CORE SATURATED (driver13 core 1 was >=95% busy in 84% of 61 in-window samples — the load generator had no headroom; this rung measures the driver, not the broker)
NOT CARRIED: INVALID EVIDENCE (endpoint scrape window uncertainty exceeds 2%); DRIVER CORE SATURATED (driver13 core 1 was >=95% busy in 63% of 65 in-window samples — the load generator had no headroom; this rung measures the driver, not the broker)
NOT CARRIED: INVALID EVIDENCE (endpoint scrape window uncertainty exceeds 2%); DRIVER CORE SATURATED (driver13 core 1 was >=95% busy in 74% of 66 in-window samples — the load generator had no headroom; this rung measures the driver, not the broker)
```

- **One load-generator host had a saturated core**: `driver13`, core 1, in the opening
  arm and again in the drift control 42 minutes later. The rig rejects a rung whose
  generator had no headroom.
- **A driver host answered its window scrape slowly on each of the three rungs**: 2.6
  and 2.8 s on `driver13`, 1.7 s on `driver19`, against at most 26 ms on any broker. The
  rig's 2% bound on the drivers' endpoint windows failed, so the summarizer refused the
  audited driver evidence and fell back to the subscribers' logs. `delivered/s (drivers)` on these three rows is that fallback.
- **The two readings disagree by 3.9%, 3.1% and 3.6%**, past the tool's 2% tolerance, and
  `summarize-curve.py` prints `READINGS DISAGREE` for each of the three.
- **It was not the publishers, and it was not loss.** The publishers sent 99.9–100.0% of
  the offer (389,551, 420,029 and 390,001 msg/s; no OFFER NOT MET or PUBLISHERS LATE
  flag; 0.4–1.7% of publishes behind schedule). The brokers counted all of it received
  and delivered, and the ledger below closes on every rung. The 3–4% is a disagreement
  between a fallback driver-side reading and the brokers' counters. This card asserts the
  brokers'.

An earlier version of this card said the brokers' own window scrapes were also wider than
2% of the window. They were not. That figure was the widest scrape on any host, and the
wide ones were two driver hosts'. `extract-lane-e.py` now prints the brokers' bracket and the
band it leaves on their rate (`broker_bracket_ms`, `deliv_band`) apart from the drivers'.

## What this does and does not show

- **Holds, by the reading rule:** every rung. 40.0–50.0k msg/s per node at 3 nodes and
  39.0–42.0k at 10, with no message lost and p99 ≤ 50 ms.
- **Certified by the rig:** 120,000 msg/s at 3 nodes, twice, and 150,000 msg/s once.
  Certified here means the rig graded the rung GREEN; it is still a rung of an unreleased
  build, not a published-curve point.
- **Not certified:** every 10-node rung above 1 site. The rate previously certified at 10
  nodes (39k/node, v1.0.18) is **not** re-certified by this run.
- **Not shown:** a knee, at either size. The 3-node ladder stopped at its top rung and the
  10-node ladder stopped on the rig's verdicts.
- **One window per rung.** 60 s, one repetition except 3 nodes at 4 sites.
- **Not a comparison with v1.1.0.** No A/B ran on this provisioning.

The fix for the next run is on the generator side: replace or exclude a driver whose core
saturates, and use a longer window (`LANE_E_SECS=120`) so the endpoint scrapes fit the 2%
bound.

## Reproduce

```sh
cd bench/scale
export HCLOUD_TOKEN=...          # a dedicated Hetzner project, 30 servers / 200 vCPU
set -a && . qos1-campaign/confirm-knees-7d4eced.env && set +a
export QOS1_DRIVER_ARCHIVE=/absolute/path/to/driver.tar.gz   # qos1-driver/build.sh <dir>
PREFLIGHT_ONLY=1 ./482-per-node-knee.sh   # offline
./482-per-node-knee.sh                    # PAID: 10 -> 3 -> 10 on 30 servers, ~1.5 h

# gate each arm, then read it
python3 extract-lane-e.py --crossing-gate 0.5 .runs/knee-<stamp>/<arm>/results
python3 summarize-curve.py .runs/knee-<stamp>/<arm>/results   # the rig's verdicts, then the broker-counter reading
python3 qos1-campaign/ledger-report.py .runs/knee-<stamp>/<arm>
python3 chart-scale-out.py qos1-candidate   # redraw the chart from the recorded points
```
